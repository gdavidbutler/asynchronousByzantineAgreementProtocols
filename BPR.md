# BPR -- Bracha Phase Retry

Reliable delivery for counting-threshold broadcast over fair-loss channels, placed at the protocol endpoint.

This document is the governing statement for the stratum beneath the papers' reliable-channel assumption: BPR itself, its retire gates and suppress masks, the announcements and the annotation exchange, the sweep-side pacing of the composition's two duty seams, and the abandonment-policy shape. No published paper covers this machinery, so this document stands in for one, at the papers' own abstraction level: the mechanism is stated in protocol vocabulary, each gate carries its argument, and the code bridges to this document the way it bridges to Bracha87.txt and BKR94ACS.txt. The headers remain the API reference; *Realization in This Library* at the end maps each concept to its entry points.

The posture is stated up front and never exceeded: nothing here is theorem-grade. Each gate stands on its own argument, and this repository weighs exactly two sources -- the papers, and its own testing. For what the papers cannot see, the test batteries are the review of record (*The Review of Record* below), and every claim is bounded by *The Scoped-Claim Registry*.

## The Gap

Bracha's correctness proofs presume a reliable message system (Section 1, the model; extracted in Bracha87.txt):

> "We assume a reliable message system in which no messages are lost or generated. Each process can directly send messages to any other process, and can identify the sender of every message it receives."

That assumption decomposes into three obligations. Two remain the transport's: no fabrication (the transport must not invent messages a process never sent) and sender identification (a Byzantine process must not impersonate a correct one) -- in practice, authenticated point-to-point channels. The third -- every message sent between correct processes eventually arrives -- is not satisfied by fair-loss datagrams, where a message may be silently dropped any finite number of times in transit. BPR closes exactly that third obligation: it converts "each send may be lost finitely often" into "each owed send is re-sent until retired." A transport that drops a message infinitely often is outside the model and outside every claim in this document.

The closure preserves asynchrony. No wall-clock predicate appears anywhere in the mechanism: the application's retry tick IS the event, silent ticks output nothing, and the rate of retry calls bounds retry volume. The papers' correctness claims -- safety, and probabilistic termination -- are proven under arbitrary asynchrony, and BPR adds no timing assumption to them.

## Placement

BPR is the end-to-end argument (Saltzer/Reed/Clark 1984; SRC84.txt) applied to Bracha. The reliability function -- "deliver INITIAL/ECHO/READY despite a fair-loss network until the protocol no longer owes them" -- depends on a "still owed" predicate, and the complete knowledge behind that predicate lives only at the protocol endpoint: the sent-action flags (echoed, ready-sent), the initiator bit, the announcement evidence READY's retire reads (*Suppression and the Announcements*), and -- at the composition layer -- the per-process BA-decided state. A separate stubborn-link layer below cannot decide when to retire without being told by the protocol, which folds the function back into the endpoint anyway (SRC84's E1 correctness argument; P3(b), the lower layer has less information). So the retry runs over exactly the protocol's own state, and the application maintains no parallel structure of its own -- no destination masks, no per-process receipt tracking, no per-instance delivery clocks (the sweep-side patience counters are pacing, not delivery state -- *The Sweep-Side Decisions*); the masks and evidence the broadcast consults are library-owned protocol state, carried on messages the protocol already sends and surfaced for the caller to honor.

Two boundary facts about the citation:

- **SRC84 licenses placement only.** It argues where the retry lives and nothing else; no retire gate's soundness follows from it. Each gate stands on its own argument below.
- **Lower-layer optimizations remain admissible** under SRC84's performance carve-outs (P1, P2): wire FEC, batching, inter-shard delay are tunables that bound retry frequency, never substitutes for the correctness obligation.

## The Retry

**State.** Retry state is intrinsic to the protocol: the sent flags (echoed, ready-sent) plus a one-bit initiator flag naming this process as the broadcast's designated initiator, the per-sender bitmaps the instance already keeps (who echoed, who readied) and the announcement evidence (who announced an accept, who is armed), and at the composition layer the per-process BA decision byte. Everything below is derived from these.

**Event model.** The retry is reactive: rules fire only when called. One Retry call per tick is the caller discipline -- retrying on every delivered message floods the wire at message rate, and retrying only on idle starves under a steady honest trickle; the tick is a wire rate limit, paced in wall time precisely because correctness does not depend on its accuracy.

**Units.** A *tick* is one Retry call per process. A *sweep* is one full pass of the retry cursor over every sent Fig 1 instance, read off the cursor's own wrap count -- compared, never assumed to advance by one, since a single call can complete two passes. Counting Retry calls against the sent-instance count instead closes the pass late: the cursor walks past an instance whose retries have retired without spending a call on it, while the count still includes it, so the error is the retired count and it grows as a run matures. The sweep is the budget unit for everything deliberate in this stratum -- the patience at the duty seams and the barren-sweep abandon gate -- because a sweep is what re-sends every sent instance exactly once: a budget denominated in calls re-sends only its own count out of the sweep length and shrinks in real terms over a run.

**Composition with the paper rules.** BPR rules consume the paper rules' outputs and sent-state flags as inputs and produce distinct retry outputs; the chain is one-directional -- BPR cannot affect a paper decision -- so an audit of paper fidelity reads only the paper rules without considering any retry rule.

## Retirement

The retirement principle is *minimal retry*: each sent action retires at the soonest point it is provably no longer owed to ANY correct process. The gates follow from the three message types' roles.

**Roles.** INITIAL and ECHO are bootstrap-only: they exist to drive t+1 correct processes to send READY, after which ready-amplification (t+1 readys induce a ready; 2t+1 readys accept) is self-sustaining and consumes neither. READY is the amplification carrier: it is exactly what the amplification tail consumes, and retry is the only delivery mechanism the protocol has for getting it there under loss. The load-bearing amplification fact: ACCEPTED witnesses 2t+1 readys, of which at least t+1 are correct and retried until every process has accepted and holds the retrier's accept, so every correct process eventually reaches accept on readys alone.

**INITIAL** (initiator only) retires at ACCEPTED, or at all-echoed (an echo observed from every one of the n processes). ACCEPTED establishes the t+1 correct readys that finish the protocol without any INITIAL; all-echoed leaves nothing for an INITIAL to induce, since INITIAL only induces echoes.

**INITIAL may be held before it is ever sent**, and a hold is not a retire. An initiator that has stored its value but withholds the broadcast (`bracha87Fig1Hold`) has nothing on the wire to re-send, so the retry row reads "held" and stays silent until the release (`bracha87Fig1Release`), after which INITIAL retries and retires as above. The hold exists for one message: the Fig 4 step-1 broadcast that carries a process's coin, which the composition withholds at the turn when the deployment chooses it at `bkr94acsInit` and releases on the caller's word (`bkr94acsBaReveal`), so that a caller with a local coin, able to bound when every correct step-3 turn of the phase has fired, can keep any coin from reaching a faulty process before then. A global coin hosted behind `bracha87CoinFn` has nothing to keep from a faulty process, whose own closure already names it, and is deployed without it. What it costs and cannot cost is argued on its own ground under *The Sweep-Side Decisions*, the hold; a caller that never releases is, to every other process, silent at that round, as the contract states.

**ECHO** retires at ACCEPTED, by the same amplification argument: past t+1 correct readys, no process consumes an echo.

**The forbidden weaker gate** is "stop INITIAL once locally echoed." At the n = 3t+1 boundary the echo threshold (n+t)/2 + 1 equals the count of honest processes, so at local-echo time no readys may exist yet -- the rescue set is not established -- and an honest process that missed the bootstrap can be left one echo short forever; only the initiator can break it. Both retained stops are strictly stronger than local echo. Under up to t silent processes all-echoed is unreachable, so that path self-disables and ACCEPTED alone carries the retirement.

**READY never retires on local state.** An accepted process still owes its READY to processes below the 2t+1 threshold -- local accept is evidence about this process, and the carrier is owed to the others. The "ACCEPTED, so stop retrying READY" optimization strands slow processes; it is the asymmetry with the bootstrap gates: ACCEPTED retires INITIAL and ECHO but must never retire READY.

**READY retires only on remote evidence, held for every process:** it has announced its accept, AND its re-sends arrive marked RECEIVED -- the second fact recorded only as the absence of re-arming, since a READY arriving unmarked is its sender showing this process's accept has not been received there (*Suppression and the Announcements*). "q has accepted" and "q has received mine" are different facts, and only the READY this process is already re-sending can make the second true. Suppressing on the first alone silences the announcement q has never received, so q's own gate stands one bit short forever -- a state reachable with no loss at all, by nothing more than a cursor phase offset. When that evidence holds for all n, no process consumes a ready anywhere and no process is owed an announcement: genuine quiescence, the retry's success-side ending. Below all n, the same evidence drops READY per process.

## Suppression and the Announcements

The whole-action retires above are all-or-nothing across n recipients. Each retry action also carries a per-process suppress mask naming the processes that provably no longer consume it, so the broadcast drops a fast process the moment IT crosses, not when the last process does:

- INITIAL is suppressed to processes that have echoed (nothing left to induce there);
- ECHO is suppressed to processes that have sent READY -- readied, not merely echoed, because an echoed-but-not-readied process still consumes echoes toward its ready threshold;
- READY is suppressed to processes for which the remote pair of facts holds: accepted, and having received this instance's accept.

READY's per-process retire point is wire-silent in base Bracha -- nothing in the paper's messages says "I have accepted" -- so the mechanism adds two annotations that ride the READY the protocol is already retrying, costing no additional messages:

**The accept announcement.** An accepted process marks its outgoing READYs; the receiver records the announcing sender as accepted. The announcement is recorded after the READY's normal input processing, so the accepted set stays a subset of the readied set. A process is announced to itself the same way, by its own READY hand-back carrying the annotation, so the quiescence count reaches n; nothing is written from local state -- written at the accept itself, whenever READY and ACCEPT fire on one message (t = 0), the self-accept would suppress the hand-back before it is framed and the readied set would never gain self.

**The RECEIVED annotation.** A READY carrying the RECEIVED annotation says "your accept was received here"; one arriving without it shows the opposite -- its sender would have suppressed this process had it received the accept, so the unmarked re-send is the sender showing it has not. An unmarked READY un-suppresses its sender for the next READY re-send, which goes out marked wherever that sender's own announcement has been recorded -- toward a sender not yet recorded it goes out unmarked, and that sender was never suppressed. A marked READY re-arms nothing -- the annotation is the discriminator that keeps the exchange from ping-ponging; without it every READY is byte-identical to every other and the honest run never falls silent. The arm is a row of the Fig 1 dispatch, which runs on every arrival -- a re-sent READY is a duplicate, and the paper's outputs are finished with it, the BPR rows are not -- read off `bracha87Fig1Input`'s second annotation argument as the announcement is off its first (one `annot` byte at the composition) and gated on this process having accepted: before that nothing is owed, so an unmarked READY records its ready and its sender's announcement if carried, and arms nothing; the READY that carries the accept itself takes its own arm through the chain on accept(v), the caller ordering nothing.

**Caller obligation.** A caller that drops an instance from its retry rotation on the quiescent 0 return must put it back when an unmarked READY arrives. Only a tick can re-send, and an unmarked READY is exactly the evidence that something is still owed; skipping the re-entry costs nothing while nothing is lost, and forfeits the whole fair-loss recovery the moment a marked re-send is dropped -- its target then re-sends unmarked forever into a process that has stopped listening. Both this forfeit and its neighbor -- a caller that never carries RECEIVED re-arms every post-accept READY and never converges -- are driven in the black-box H3 lanes: a placed loss of one process's announcement toward another until the first parks, with the parker sweeping faster (lock-step equal sweeps re-arm it every pass and it never parks), stranding the other without re-entry and recovering with it; and RECEIVED stripped from every READY with ACCEPTED still carried, so what holds every mask open is the arm.

**Byzantine safety of the annotations.** A forged accept announcement marks only its own sender: it retires this process's retry toward the liar -- which harms only the liar -- and can never strand a correct laggard, whose bit is set solely by its own true announcement. The all-n quiescence gate requires every CORRECT process's true accept regardless of what up to t Byzantine processes claim. No count-threshold shortcut ("2t+1 announced, stop") is admissible: up to t forgeries plus a partial correct set could trip it while a correct process is still below its ready threshold. A forged unmarked READY is equally contained: an arm only un-suppresses its own sender, so the forger buys one masked READY per instance per pass of the receiving process's cursor, aimed at itself -- the same standing an announcement it never sends already has, and at a strictly higher cost, since silence buys it for free -- and displaces nothing owed to a correct process.

The price is paid at the RECEIVING process's own pass rate, and a forger cannot raise it by re-sending faster. Two separate facts carry that, and only the second is about the annotation. The RATE is the retry cursor's cadence: the walk visits an instance once per pass and one visit emits at most one READY action, so any instance that still owes anything is served exactly that often, forgery or no forgery -- what an arm buys is that the instance still owes, never how often it is answered. The DEPTH is the arm's representation: it is a bitmap, not a counter, so however many unmarked re-sends arrive between one egress for an instance and the next, that egress consumes them all and answers once, and the surplus is forfeited rather than deferred. Stopping the forger is what makes the difference visible -- the residue then owes one more masked READY per armed instance and falls silent, at a depth its rate did not buy.

The two lies do not compound, and the announcement lie on its own buys nothing lasting. Suppression is per recipient and it is the honest processes that apply it, so a forger that announces an accept it does not hold is dropped from their READY recipient sets -- but its own machine, genuinely lacking their accepts, then re-sends unmarked toward each of them, each such re-send arms its receiver, and the receiver's next egress carries the announcement. The second annotation restores the flow it was built to restore, one pass late. Measured at n=7 with one such liar and a long further drive: the liar ends short on exactly the instance every honest process is short on, the decided-0 one the retry gate skips. An otherwise-honest machine that tells this lie converges like any other and quiesces. Holding a gate open therefore takes the other thing an authenticated attacker chooses -- multiplicity, re-sending its own authentic READY unmarked, indefinitely -- and that is the shape the price above is stated for.

## Quiescence

Quiescence is the state where every process has accepted and none is owed an announcement -- the READY suppress evidence covering all n -- at which point the retry owes nothing to anyone and a full sweep returns 0 (a held phase-opening INITIAL, *Retirement*, initiated but not yet on the wire, is the one thing a sweep returning 0 can still owe). At the composition layer the per-process verdict gate bounds the reading: a decided-0 instance's evidence is not owed and may stay short for good (*The Composition Layer*), so the all-n evidence covers the instances the retry still serves. It is the success-side retire of the retry itself, distinct from the protocol's success signals (which are not stops) and from abandonment (which is the policy's exit).

Under fair loss quiescence is REACHABLE, not guaranteed: a lost marked READY leaves its target's evidence unchanged, so that process's unmarked re-sends persist at its own tick rate, each re-arms, each draws the marked re-send again, and fair loss delivers one eventually. What can block the gate forever is a residue no annotation can reach. The honest class is the process that leaves before its accept announcement ever goes out -- one that abandons early, or never announces at all; it is safe, because it can persist only once every correct process has accepted, and its cost is an unconsumed READY retry, not a correctness loss. A Byzantine process that announced and then keeps re-arming holds the gate open at the price already named in *Suppression and the Announcements* -- one masked READY per instance per pass of the receiving cursor, aimed at itself, nothing owed to a correct process displaced. The scope matters in both directions: a process that announces its accept and THEN leaves lets the others quiesce -- their evidence fills on the announcement and nothing ever re-arms -- so the honest residue class is exactly the never-announcer. The application's abandonment policy remains the backstop for it.

Quiescence is per-instance evidence, not a return value. The ending claim -- this instance has retired READY -- is its announcement evidence, net of outstanding arms, covering all n, and the sweep's 0 return is a weaker derived fact: a machine carrying the forbidden local-accept retire produces the same 0. A checker reads the evidence; it does not infer it.

## The Composition Layer

The ACS composition owns all its Fig 1 instances (n A-Cast instances; BAs by process x round x initiator), and its retry walks them with a single cursor, outputting one instance's retries per call -- which is what makes the sweep a well-defined unit there.

The composition adds one gate of its own, the **per-process verdict gate**, decided from the local BA state for the A-Cast instance being walked:

- *undecided* -> retry: other honest processes may still learn Q(j) = 1, and their Fig 1 needs these echoes and readys;
- *decided 1* -> retry: post-decide continuation -- processes that have not yet observed the accept still need the traffic, else their BA can go to 0 via step 2 and cross-process SubSet agreement breaks;
- *exhausted* -> retry: no decision was made; other processes may still benefit from earlier-round traffic;
- *decided 0* -> skip: the process is excluded from SubSet at every correct process (BA agreement), so nobody needs its A-Cast; this process's own enter, 0 by step 2 or 1 by step 1 before the BA went the other way, is spent. The gate itself is the retire: completion of the announcement evidence is not owed for a gated instance, and its evidence may stay short for good. The gate outranks the annotation exchange.

The "still owed" predicate at this layer therefore combines Bracha's own retry state -- the sent flags and the per-sender bitmaps its gates read -- with the BA-decided state; both live at this endpoint, which is the placement argument applied a second time.

## The Sweep-Side Decisions

In the papers' asynchronous model (unbounded finite delay, no clocks) a rule's "upon" / "wait until" names the evidence that ENABLES an action, never the moment it is taken -- the model has no moments. Two of the composition's decisions consume evidence that is still growing when it first suffices, so their firing is paced by the caller from the retry sweep, the deployment's only time-like notion. Both classify with one trichotomy:

- **HELD** -- not enabled: firing now would be unsound or is impossible; elapsed time is irrelevant.
- **TOLERANCE** -- enabled, and waiting could still improve the outcome; the caller counts completed sweeps against its patience and fires when it elapses.
- **MET** -- nothing left to wait for; firing is free. The turn's MET is enabled with the round's validated count at n; the fanout's is every process entered, which makes its firing moot, and it is reached whatever the count.

**Enabling and firing are two events, and the papers name only the first.** "Upon" and "wait until" state the evidence that enables a step; with no moments in the model, the papers never place the firing, and their proofs read as if the two were one event. This library separates them. Enabled is the duty leaving HELD. The firing is the fanout at seam 1 and the turn at seam 2, each placed by the caller; the interval between is the patience, spent in sweeps, and MET is nothing left to wait for, the firing still the caller's. Both a firing at enabling and a paced firing are correct with up to t faulty processes, on each seam's own ground, and both grounds are this repository's readings of the proofs rather than the papers' words: the fanout's is that BKR94's Lemma 2 proof asks of the fanout only that the 0-inputs are eventually entered once the count holds (*Seam 1*), and the turn's is that Bracha's Fig 4 proofs hold for any sample of at least n-t validated messages, where the paper writes "the n - t" (*Seam 2*). The paced firing is argued the more correct of the two: a deployment's delay is bounded in practice while the model's is not, and patience spends some of that practical bound on the re-sends that carry an honest laggard's A-Cast in before the fanout closes the subset without it, at a liveness cost only. The proofs never depend on the bound. *At enabling* is the name the rest of this repository uses for the first schedule, and it defers here.

A zero patience is not by itself a firing at enabling: it spends no deliberate wait, provided the count is tested against the patience on every attempt -- a clock advanced only at sweep boundaries fires one boundary late even at zero. It does not by itself name a sample. The papers prescribe no schedule, so the smallest legal one -- the n-t'th validation -- is a reading, one of a range the proofs cover whole, and where the caller calls from is what reaches it: draining as evidence is banked consumes that validation and whatever a cascade banked beside it, while calling once per sweep consumes whatever had validated by then, a superset. Both are proof-covered, and the difference is coin luck, never safety.

**Seam 1 -- the step-2 fanout.** BKR94's rule: upon 2t+1 BA outputs of 1, enter 0 into every unentered BA -- where this library compares against the larger n-t throughout the supported range, firing strictly later on more evidence (README.md, Implementation Note 15). (The trigger is BA-outputs-1, never "n-t accepts" -- the two diverge under asynchrony and only the former satisfies the paper's Lemma 2 Part A case (i).) Fired at the instant the local count crosses, the fastest n-t processes close SubSet against every honest process whose A-Cast is still in flight. While patience is consumed, the same sweeps are re-carrying the delayed A-Casts, so the wait is spent on exactly the recovery that can make the firing unnecessary.

**Seam 2 -- the BA round turn.** Bracha's Fig 4 steps each wait on n-t validations of their own round ("Wait until validate n - t (3i+1)-messages", and its 3i+2 and 3i+3 counterparts) and then compute over that set, which keeps growing past n-t; the proofs are read here as holding for ANY sample of at least n-t (the opening paragraph of this section), so the sample a round consumes is purely a function of when the turn fires. The paper's proofs are written for exactly n-t (Bracha87.txt, the Section 5 proofs), and the detail the implementation reads out of them is that n-t plays two roles there. One is Fig 3's witness set, the n-t messages of VALID^{k-1} a validated k-message must be computable from. This library holds it at n-t whatever the turn consumes, and when more than n-t messages are in hand fig4Nfn (bracha87.c) answers Fig 3's existential over n-t subsets, so a value computed over the larger sample validates wherever some n-t subset yields it. The other is the sample the turn consumes, and the proofs ask of it only two things a larger sample keeps: that correct copies hold its majority, s - t > s/2 for every s > 2t, and that it omits at most t of the n messages, n - s <= t for every s >= n-t. The step tests themselves are absolute thresholds, more than n/2 at 3i+2 and more than 2t or more than t at 3i+3, never fractions of the sample. The arrival path only banks evidence; each BA's next round is computed from the sweep. The cost of firing at enabling here is coin luck, not exclusion -- a late message still validates the next round -- which is why the turn's patience is pure tuning while the fanout's guards fairness. At the decide step the gain is one-directional (the decide/adopt counts are monotone thresholds; a fuller sample can only convert coin phases into deterministic decides); the 3i+2 step's test counts the sample against the paper's own more-than-n/2 threshold, denominated in n rather than in the sample, which bracha87.c matches, so it is a monotone threshold too; the phase-opening majority is a comparison a fuller sample can flip, and both outcomes are proof-covered, so the flip trades between sound broadcasts, never against safety.

**The safety license, and its one-sidedness.** Deferring an enabled firing costs liveness only, never safety -- on each seam's own ground. The turn's ground is the sample: a paced turn consumes a superset of the sample a turn at enabling consumes, and the proofs are read here as holding for any sample of at least n-t. The fanout's ground is the reading of the Lemma 2 proof above: the proof asks of the fanout only that the 0-inputs are eventually entered once the count holds, and the count is monotone. The license covers exactly these two seams: step-1 enter-1 fires on arrival, and no pacing claim attaches to it.

**The hold, a third seam, licensed on its own ground.** It defers something the other two do not: not a firing but a send whose value is already computed -- the phase-opening INITIAL of a Fig 4 BA, the one message of a phase that carries a process's coin, which `bkr94acsTurn` withholds (`bracha87Fig1Hold`) when `bkr94acsInit` was given the hold, and `bkr94acsBaReveal` releases on the caller's word. Its ground is the model's own asynchrony. To every receiver a held-then-released INITIAL is a message the network delayed, and Section 1 puts no bound on message delays; the lemmas the composition's safety rests on -- Lemmas 1, 2, 5 and 10, and the counting half of Theorem 2's agreement, that every correct process holds the decided value once any has decided -- are proved by counting over messages received, and Lemma 6 by induction on k, base and step each closed by Theorem 1's "q will eventually accept m"; none reads when a message was sent, so no two correct processes decide differently whatever a hold does. What reads the hold is liveness, and the paper's validity with it: Validity is proved "by Lemma 9" and nothing else, and agreement closes on Lemma 9 too, so the decisions they promise are the liveness the release buys. Lemma 8's proof takes every correct process to have broadcast at the first blocked round, and with a hold in the machine having reached a round no longer implies having broadcast at it (a held process turns its later rounds on the others' INITIALs); the proof, and Lemma 9's step through it, carry under one added premise, that every hold is released. So a hold costs liveness only, and the release is what a caller owes to stay inside the theorems: a process that never releases is, to every other correct process, silent at every phase-opening round -- a transmitter no one can tell from a crashed one -- and spends one of the t at each of them, so that with t genuine faults silent every correct process blocks at that round, the holder included. The unit is the sweep, as at the other two seams (the bundled example's `-r` counts completed passes under its local coin), and the seam's place is before abandon under the turn's rule: the reveal's window opens act-less, so holding its patience under the abandon gate is the caller's obligation, not structural. The license is one-sided here as everywhere: WHEN to release is the caller's, and releasing earlier than a discipline says forfeits only the coin's cover, never a proof.

**Budget discipline.** Both deliberate quantities are sweep-denominated (the unit definitions are under *The Retry*), the two clocks advance on the same boundaries, and waiting is not progress -- so a patience that has not elapsed strictly before the abandonment gate would fire lands its decision in a caller that is already leaving. At the step-2 fanout, an abandon gate sized above the patience makes that ordering structural: the window opens on the n-t'th BA output of 1, which is a decision the caller counts as progress, so the sweep the window opens in is not barren and the barren count stands at zero on the boundary that first charges the patience, trailing it by at least one sweep from there. At the BA round turn the ordering is caller discipline and nothing more: sizing abandon above the patience is necessary -- a gate at or below it can fire before the BA turns -- and it is not sufficient, because that window opens act-less at t >= 1 (the accepting delivery that completes a round returns no actions), so the barren count at its opening is whatever the run left there, and the turn's duty is not monotone across rounds. At t = 0 neither question arises: the turn's TOLERANCE band [n-t, n) is empty by arithmetic, and the fanout's emptiness is the protocol's rather than the arithmetic's -- at t = 0 a BA cannot output 1 while unentered, since deciding it requires this process's own round-0 INITIAL, which is the entering. Patience is scoped to undecided BAs: post-decide continuation rounds have nothing left to choose (Lemma 9 fixes every sample they can meet) while their turns feed the next process's round, so holding them to a patience convoys the whole cohort.

**The unit is local.** Sweep-denominated patience counts the FIRING process's completed passes while a delayed instance is re-carried at the cursor's rate, so it is not fairness-invariant across sustained rate skew: the same honest A-Cast a patience includes at matched rates is excluded when the firing processes sweep faster, their patience elapsing inside less recovery. Claimed: patience buys one re-send of every sent instance per counted sweep. Not claimed: that a fixed patience admits the same processes across heterogeneous sweep rates.

## Termination and Abandonment

Under arbitrary asynchrony the protocol can give the application evidence of progress; it can never give evidence of death or evidence that stopping is safe. A dead process and a slow one present the same evidence stream, and under unbounded latency no process can know that another no longer needs its messages. What remains is exactly one sound policy shape: watch the evidence of progress and give up -- **abandon** -- after enough consecutive sweeps without any.

A run has exactly one exit -- abandon -- and the markers on the way there are not stops. EXHAUSTED (a BA consumed its round encoding) means that BA will never decide and completion is unreachable; the loop keeps draining and ticking, no unilateral substitute decision is permitted, and the event is surfaced as the failure cause when the gate fires. COMPLETE is the success marker; post-decide continuation requires broadcasting past it, so even a successful run leaves through the gate unless the retry itself first runs out of work -- quiescence, which is no exit decision at all but the owing ending (*Quiescence*).

**Progress is narrow, and the narrowness is load-bearing.** Progress is an input that returns actions (duplicate deliveries -- including every retransmission -- return nothing, which is what makes a progress counter stable under retry noise), a decision, or a completion, plus any application-level first-arrivals the deployment chooses to count. Never the retry egress, and never the sweep-side machinery's routine sends: post-decide continuation outputs BA sends every sweep until the round space is spent, so a wider definition holds the gate open against a correct machine.

**The gate counts sweeps, never wall time.** A sweep that ends with no progress observed is *barren*; the gate fires after S consecutive barren sweeps, and reaching COMPLETE first is the success flavor of the same question. S remains the gate's own parameter, but it is not sized on its own ground: the deployment turns one knob, the patience the duty seams consume, and S derives from it (*The Abandon Boundary*). Wall time has exactly one legitimate role, pacing the tick; driving abandonment off wall time encodes a synchrony bound the protocol does not assume and silently converts a high-latency deployment into a failing one. Counting sweeps makes the policy commensurate with the protocol: a slow run takes more wall time per sweep but the same number of sweeps. Worst-case time to the gate is computable in advance -- S x sweep length x the tick's interval -- and the sweep length is bounded by the instance space.

**Evidence is local by construction.** The policy reads only the local stream, and asynchrony makes opposite local outcomes simultaneously correct. A partitioned process sees exactly what an arbitrarily slow one sees -- nothing fresh, barren count climbing -- so the policy must not try to distinguish them; the side still holding n-t correct processes runs to completion without it -- its A-Cast included or excluded by how far it had spread before the cut, the survivors agreeing either way -- and if the cut heals while survivors still tick, their never-retired READY carries the returner to the same subset (READY alone suffices: the value rides with it and the t+1-readys rule re-bootstraps the missed INITIAL and ECHO). Under an asymmetric cut, a receive-only process can run to COMPLETE with the agreed subset -- its own unheard A-Cast excluded everywhere, including at itself -- while everyone else counts it silent, and a send-only process feeds everyone -- its A-Cast possibly agreed on by all -- while itself observing pure barrenness and abandoning; both behaved correctly, and no protocol signal exists to reconcile them.

**The Byzantine stretch is bounded.** Up to t silent processes cost only the retry tail -- every all-n gate stays open toward them until abandonment, which is correct, since a silent process is indistinguishable from a laggard that still needs the traffic and per-process suppression has already dropped everyone who crossed. The mirror threat aims at the gate itself: a Byzantine process feeding genuinely fresh acts that lead nowhere, each resetting the barren count. Its supply is finite -- per-sender dedup admits one echo and one ready per sender per instance, and the instance space is finite -- so up to t such processes stretch the gate but cannot hold it open. The bound rests on dedup being value-blind: a repeated sender registers nothing regardless of content bytes, so a same-sender echo carrying different content contributes nothing; a dedup keyed on (sender, content) reopens the supply. The stretch is part of the adversary's allowance: it defers the gate -- each fresh act resets the barren count -- and the finite supply bounds the deferral; the threat lands in the policy, never in agreement.

**Staggered start is the realistic case, not an edge.** A process that starts late is, until its first message, byte-identical to a dead one -- and the others' retries are precisely the bootstrap it missed. The only thing that can kill a legitimately late run is the abandon gate itself; patience costs wall time only, and a gate derived from too little patience converts a slow deployment into a failed one.

## The Abandon Boundary

Abandonment is the single exit, and everything deliberate this stratum does about time sits strictly inside it. The patience windows at the two duty seams are proceed-without decisions taken before abandon, denominated in the same sweeps the barren gate counts, and covered by the one-sided safety license; patience that has not elapsed strictly before the abandon gate fires its decision into a caller that is already leaving. That ordering is what sizes the gate. The deployment turns one knob -- the patience, in full cursor passes -- and abandon derives from it: S is twice the patience. One patience is the window itself, the passes that re-carry the delayed A-Casts; the same again covers the rounds the 0-entered BAs still take, which, entered last, run their INITIAL and READY exchanges while the rest of the cohort has long since readied -- work bounded by the same delay notion the patience already encodes. How a deployment converts its environment into passes is its own business and enters this stratum nowhere. At the step-2 fanout S above the patience makes the ordering structural, because that window opens on a BA output of 1 and the caller counts a BA decision as progress: the barren count stands at zero on the boundary that first charges the patience and trails it by a sweep from there, and the doubling leaves a full patience of slack at every patience of one pass or more. At the BA round turn S above the patience is necessary and not sufficient -- that window opens act-less -- so holding the turn's patience under the gate stays the caller's obligation. The derivation speaks for a patience of one pass or more: twice zero is not an abandon sizing, and a deployment that spends no patience at either seam sizes S on its own ground. Quiescence is the retry's own success-side retire, not a policy exit. The hold is the third deferral this stratum takes, and it meets the three: its patience is in passes, its place is the turn's (before abandon, by the caller's ordering), and its argument is its own (*The Sweep-Side Decisions*, the hold). Any further deliberate deferral this stratum ever takes owes the same three things before it exists: a place strictly before abandon, the sweep as its unit, and its own deferral-costs-liveness-only argument on the grade of the two above -- a superset of the sample taken at enabling, or a proof that consumes only eventual firing -- or it gets no safety license at all.
