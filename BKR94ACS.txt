Ben-Or, Kelmer, Rabin 1994 "Asynchronous Secure Computations with
Optimal Resilience (Extended Abstract)"
PODC '94, pages 183-192

Scope of this extract: Section 4 ("Agreement on a Common Subset"),
plus the Section 2 facts (communication and adversary model) that bear
on an ACS caller. The paper's secret sharing (USS/AVSS), linear
combination, multiplication, Equality ZK Proof machinery, and the
overall MPC construction are unrelated to ACS as a standalone
primitive and are deliberately not summarized here.


Model and tools
---------------

n players (processors), fully connected asynchronous network, up to
t Byzantine faults. The MPC construction targets optimal resilience
 t < n/3 (i.e. n >= 3t + 1).

Channel and adversary model (paper Section 2): every two players are
connected by a secure and reliable channel -- a message on it cannot
be heard by other players, is guaranteed to arrive, and may be
delayed arbitrarily (finitely). There is NO broadcast primitive in
the model; A-Cast is a simulation over the pairwise channels
(Bracha 1984). The adversary is computationally unbounded and
dynamic: it may corrupt up to t players adaptively at any time
(taking their private data and their control), and it schedules the
delay of every message, but it can neither read nor alter messages
between honest players.

Termination caveat, and where it does NOT attach: the paper notes its
MPC (and the Canetti-Rabin 1993 AVSS it builds on) carries an
exponentially small but non-zero probability of not terminating, and
states that this is unavoidable for AVSS. The paper gives the bound
two ways. Section 1: "if n < 4t then any t-resilient asynchronous
verifiable secret sharing protocol A must have some probability qA > 0
of not terminating", and "A sketch of the proof appears in Section 6."
Section 6 opens "In this section we prove that if n ~ 4i" -- the
scan's tilde and "4i" -- and its sketch runs at "n = 4, t= 1", which
is n = 4t, so the strict form would exclude the paper's own worked
example. Read the Section 6 relation as n <= 4t, the bound the proof
instantiates. That caveat attaches to the secret-sharing side, not to
ACS. The paper draws the contrast itself -- this "should be contrasted
with the asynchronous Byzantine Agreement problem where the randomized
protocol terminates with probability 1."

ACS takes one subprotocol as input:

  BA -- binary asynchronous Byzantine Agreement.  Protocol
    Agreement[Q] (Figure 3) is three steps and every one of them is
    a BA operation; the Lemma 2 proof consumes BA's correctness,
    termination and agreement properties and nothing else.  ACS is
    generic in it -- any binary BA with those properties works, and
    the construction is not part of the ACS interface.  The paper
    lists BA among its tools as "Fast Asynchronous Byzantine
    Agreement (BA) was devised by [CR93]", which is the
    instantiation BKR94's own MPC uses.

  A-Cast is NOT a subprotocol of Agreement[Q].  The paper lists it
    among the Section 2 tools -- "A simulation of an asynchronous
    broadcast channel (A-Cast) was devised by [Bra84]" -- and uses it
    on the secret-sharing side (USS-Share).  Section 4 does not
    mention it.  What Agreement[Q] needs instead is the second
    assumption on Q below: that every player eventually learns Q(j).
    How that learning happens is the caller's; this repo carries it
    over Bracha 1987 Figure 1 A-Casts, which is our construction, not
    the paper's.


Problem setup
-------------

A dynamic predicate Q assigns a binary value to each player. "Dynamic"
means not all players' values are assigned at the same time; when
player Pj's value Q(j) is finally assigned, every honest player
eventually learns it. The protocol assumes two facts about Q:

  1. Every honest player eventually has Q = 1.
  2. When Q(j) IS assigned, every honest player eventually learns
     the value.  The paper states it conditionally -- "When a player
     Pj is assigned his value, denoted as Q(j), it is guaranteed that
     all the players will (eventually) learn to know this value" --
     so it says nothing about a j whose value is never assigned.

Goal: the honest players agree on a subset of size at least n - t of
players for whom Q = 1.

The predicate Q is application-supplied. In BKR94's own MPC
construction, Q is "player P_j has properly shared his input" -- Q(j)
becomes 1 for honest P_j after P_j's USS-Share completes, and honest
players learn this by observing the shared state. ACS is generic in Q;
any monotone predicate that is eventually 1 for every honest player,
and whose assigned values eventually reach every honest player, works.


Protocol Agreement[Q]  (Figure 3)
---------------------------------

Code for player Pi. N BAs are run concurrently, one per player index
(BA_1 ... BA_n). Each player enters his inputs asynchronously as
evidence accumulates.

  1. For each Pj for whom you know that Q(j) = 1, participate in
     BA_j with input 1.

  2. Upon completing 2t+1 BA protocols with output 1, enter input 0
     to all BA protocols for which you haven't entered a value yet.

  3. Upon completing all n BA protocols, let your SubSet_i be the
     set of all indices j for which BA_j had output 1. Output
     SubSet_i.

NOTE: the three steps above keep the paper's own wording, but the scan
of Figure 3 is garbled and they are normalized against it: it reads
"Q(J = 1" for Q(j) = 1, "participate in W" for BA_j, "2t+ 1" for 2t+1,
"input O" for input 0, "SubSe~" for SubSet, and flattens the
subscripts ("BAj", "SubSeti").  So quote them freely as the paper's
words; only those renderings are this file's.

The "2t+1" threshold in step 2 equals n - t only at n = 3t+1. The
paper's regime is t < n/3, and its Section 4 opener writes the
relation as an inequality -- a subset "of size at least n - t >=
2t + 1" (the scan's relation glyph is a tilde) -- so above
n = 3t+1 the two part company and 2t+1 is the looser of the pair.

The paper cites [BOEY91] for a technique that runs the N BAs with an
O(log n) factor saving over naive independent execution; it does not
change the protocol's external behavior.


Lemma 2 (paper's statement, paraphrased)
----------------------------------------

Using the protocol Agreement (Figure 3), the players agree on a
common subset of size at least 2t + 1 of players Pj for whom
Q(j) = 1.  (Not verbatim: the scan reads "(Figure 9)", "szze" for
size, and "Q(i)" for Q(j).  The proof below is stated over the
honest players.)


Proof structure
---------------

The paper's proof is one paragraph.  The [Part A]..[Part D] labels
and the case (i)/(ii) split below are THIS FILE'S structuring of it,
not the paper's numbering -- the repo cites them, so they are kept
stable.  The proof consumes "the correctness property of the BA
protocol" (the paper's term), BA termination, the agreement it means
by "the properties of the Byzantine Agreement protocol" at Part C,
and the two assumptions about Q.  Nothing else.

[Part A] At least 2t+1 BA instances terminate with output 1.

  Case (i): Some honest player Pi entered a 0 value to some BA in
    step 2. The precondition of step 2 is "2t+1 BAs have already
    terminated with output 1" -- done.

  Case (ii): No honest player ever entered 0. Then for each honest
    player Ph (assumption Q(h)=1 eventually), every honest player's
    input to BA_h is 1 (step 1). By the correctness property of the BA
    protocol, the output of each such BA_h is 1.
    There are at least n - t honest players, so at least n - t BAs
    output 1 -- the paper writes the count as 2t+1, which n - t meets
    at n = 3t+1 and exceeds above it.

[Part B] All n BA instances terminate.

  By Part A, at least 2t+1 BAs output 1. By step 2 of the protocol,
  every honest player therefore enters an input (either 1 from step 1
  or 0 from step 2) to every BA. By BA's termination property, every
  BA terminates.

[Part C] All honest players agree on SubSet.

  After step 3 each honest player's SubSet has size >= 2t+1 (by Part
  A). BA's agreement property says every BA_j delivers the same
  output to every honest player, so every honest player has the same
  SubSet.

[Part D] For each j in SubSet, Q(j) = 1.

  The paper's whole argument: "if BAj terminated with output 1, it
  must be that at least one honest player entered 1 as his input to
  BAj."  Step 1 is the only way an honest player does that, and step 1
  fires on knowing Q(j) = 1.


Remarks for implementers
------------------------

- The protocol is expressed in terms of what a single honest player
  does as events happen. Nothing is scheduled; all actions are
  triggered by completions (learning Q(j)=1, however the caller
  carries that; BA terminations). The three steps describe "input to
  BA_j when you learn Q(j)=1", "input 0 to still-open BAs after 2t+1
  1-outputs", and "read outputs once all n BAs finish."

- Step 1 and step 2 can fire concurrently for different j values.
  Once a BA has received an input from Pi (1 from step 1 or 0 from
  step 2), step 1 and step 2 stop touching it -- BA semantics demand
  a single input per player.

- The 2t+1 threshold in step 2 is the paper's way of writing the
  honest count, which is n-t (the two coincide at n = 3t+1). It
  cannot be lowered: without it a mass of 0-inputs could force BA_j
  to output 0 for all j (SubSet empty). It cannot be raised to
  require unanimity either: n-t is the maximum guarantee honest
  players have in an asynchronous network (they cannot wait for the
  t faulty players).

- Lemma 2 is silent about *which* players end up in SubSet beyond
  "Q(j) = 1", and membership runs both ways.  Section 4 opens by
  noting that "all the honest players will eventually satisfy this
  property, but some faulty players may satisfy it as well."  And
  Section 2 says the players can "never even wait for more than n - t
  of the inputs to be entered to the computation.  Furthermore, the
  missing inputs are not necessarily of the faulty players" (the scan
  reads "n - tof", "comput at ion", "necessarily y of").  So
  SubSet need not contain every honest player: an honest P_h whose
  "Q(h)=1" evidence reaches fewer than n-t honest players before
  step 2 fires for them may be excluded.

- Per the paper, "To save a log n factor of running the n BAs, the
  techniques of [BOEY91] are employed." That is an optimization for
  the MPC use case where many Agreement[Q] calls happen; it does not
  change Lemma 2.
